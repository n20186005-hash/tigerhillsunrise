## The short answer

Bring warmth, light and grip. Specifically: layered warm clothing with a wind-resistant outer layer, a hat and gloves, a torch, water, shoes with tread, small cash, and any medication you need. That is the core of it. Everything else is comfort. A Tiger Hill sunrise happens in cold and dark, on uneven ground, with nothing reliable to buy at the top — so pack for those conditions rather than for a normal outing.

## Why the list looks like this

Three facts shape everything:

- **It is cold before dawn**, and you may be standing still for a long time.
- **It is dark**, and the last stretch is walked on uneven ground.
- **There is nothing dependable to buy**, so what you forget you largely do without.

Build your kit around those, and you will be comfortable rather than merely present.

## Clothing — the core of the kit

- **Base layer** that manages moisture.
- **Mid layer** for insulation.
- **Wind-resistant outer layer** — the hill is exposed.
- **Hat and gloves**, for the wait more than the walk.
- **Warm socks** and trousers you can be still in.

Dress in layers you can shed once the sun is up and the day warms, rather than one heavy coat you cannot adjust. The [sunrise weather guide](/sunrise-weather/) explains why the morning can be colder and clearer than the afternoon.

## Light and footing

- **A torch**, or a head torch so your hands stay free. A phone light works as backup, but a phone is also your camera, so do not drain it.
- **Shoes with grip.** The ground near the viewpoint is uneven, and you are walking it in the dark.
- **A spare battery or power bank** if you will photograph the sunrise.

## Water, snacks and health

- **Water** — the cold air is dry and you will want it.
- **A small snack** if you expect a long wait.
- **Your own medication**, including any inhaler or prescription, and anything for the cold. This page offers packing advice, not medical advice.

## Money and documents

- **Small notes** for any charge at the gate. The [entry fee guide](/entry-fee/) explains why we do not quote a figure and what to ask.
- **A phone** with the driver's number saved, so you can find them in the dark.
- **Do not rely on card or UPI** at a hill gate before dawn.

## If there are children with you

Children need more warmth than adults, something to do while waiting, and a clear plan for getting back together if the crowd separates you. See [sunrise with kids](/sunrise-with-kids/).

## If you walk slowly or have mobility needs

If steps or distances are hard, kit matters less than access: ask how close the vehicle can get to the standing area, and whether there is anywhere to sit. See the [accessibility guide](/accessibility/).

## For photographers

Beyond the basics, a spare battery and a lens cloth matter more than an extra lens. Ask about tripod rules on the day rather than assuming. See the [sunrise photography guide](/sunrise-photography/).

## If you are travelling light

You do not need specialist gear. A warm jacket over a couple of layers, any shoes with tread, and a phone torch will get most people through the morning comfortably. If you are packing for a whole trip and cannot carry much, prioritise in this order: a wind-resistant outer layer, something for your head and hands, a light source, and water. Everything else is a nice-to-have. If you forget something, you will likely be able to borrow or buy it in Darjeeling town the day before — but not at the viewpoint.

## A short, checkable list

Warm layers; wind-resistant outer; hat and gloves; torch; water; snack; grip shoes; small cash; medication; charged phone and power bank; driver's number saved. Lay it all out the night before, so the morning is a matter of picking it up rather than assembling it in the dark: see the [early morning departure guide](/early-morning-departure/).

## How to read this page

This is an independent, non-official, non-profit guide. It is not the district administration or the viewpoint operator, and it has no facilities to recommend. This is general packing advice for a cold, dark, outdoor morning at altitude, and not medical advice. Adjust it for your own health, your group and the season, and let the day itself set your expectations.
