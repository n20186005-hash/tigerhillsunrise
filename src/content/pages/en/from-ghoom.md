## The short answer

Ghoom is the closest of the well-known settlements to the Tiger Hill viewpoint, because it sits on the same ridge rather than lower down in Darjeeling town. From Ghoom you continue along the ridge road onto Senchal Road towards the hill. Even though the leg is short, you still arrange a vehicle in advance for a pre-dawn start — there is no reliable stream of taxis in Ghoom at that hour — and you still work back from the sunrise time for your date.

## Why Ghoom is the practical starting point

Most visitors start from Darjeeling town and drive up through Ghoom to reach the hill. If you are already at Ghoom — whether you stayed there, arrived by the railway, or are simply routing your day around the monastery and the loop — you are most of the way along the ridge already. That shortens the dawn ride, which for many people is the single most welcome saving on a cold morning.

## Getting to Ghoom

There are two common ways to be at Ghoom:

- **By road**, up from Darjeeling town or across from the surrounding area. This is how most people arrive.
- **By the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway**, the toy train, which serves Ghoom — the station's elevation is famously the highest on the line. The [Ghoom railway station guide](/ghoom-railway-station/) and the [Darjeeling toy train guide](/darjeeling-toy-train/) cover this in detail.

Whichever way you arrive, Ghoom is a stop on the journey to Tiger Hill, not the destination. The viewpoint is further along Senchal Road.

## The hop from Ghoom to the viewpoint

From Ghoom the road continues along the ridge and turns onto Senchal Road towards the sunrise point. Because it is a mountain road climbed in the dark, treat the drive as something to allow time for, not something to squeeze. The practical points are the same as any approach:

- **Book the vehicle** the day before, even for a short leg.
- **Confirm the pickup hour** at Ghoom from the sunrise time.
- **Agree the return** and the meeting point.
- **Ask about parking and entry** as separate from the fare.

The [sunrise taxi guide](/sunrise-taxi/) and the [how to reach guide](/how-to-reach/) walk through the arrangement.

## Walking the last leg

Some people ask about walking up from Ghoom. The remaining distance is on the ridge road rather than through town, and it is dark and uneven before dawn. If you plan to walk any part of it, confirm the route and the footing locally on the day, carry a torch, and do not count on a lit footpath. For most visitors, especially with children or anyone who walks slowly, a vehicle for this leg is the sensible choice.

## What to combine with a Ghoom start

The classic pairing around Ghoom is the **Batasia Loop**, the spiral railway and war memorial between Ghoom and Darjeeling, and **Ghoom Monastery** close by. Both are natural stops after sunrise on the way back. Treat them as separate places: each has its own arrangement, and being on the same road does not mean one ticket covers everything or that either is open at the moment you pass. See the [Tiger Hill to Batasia Loop route](/tiger-hill-to-batasia-loop/) and the [Ghoom Monastery guide](/ghoom-monastery/).

## Staying at Ghoom

If you would rather shorten the dawn ride by staying at Ghoom, that can work — but plan for it. Confirm your vehicle and pickup from Ghoom, and check what is open early, because Ghoom is quieter than Darjeeling town and not everything runs on a dawn schedule. The [where to stay guide](/where-to-stay/) weighs the trade-off between a shorter dawn ride and being in the busier town.

## Fitting Ghoom into the wider morning

A neat version of the day runs: leave Ghoom before dawn, watch the sunrise at Tiger Hill, then stop at the monastery and the loop on the way back before breakfast. The [half-day itinerary](/half-day-itinerary/) times this out.
