## The short answer

Do not look for a fixed departure hour, because there is not one. Work backwards from the sunrise time for your date: sunrise, minus the drive up, minus a buffer for the queue at the gate. Write that hour down, set two alarms, and confirm it with your driver the night before. The single most common mistake is underestimating the last stretch and the queue, and missing first light by ten minutes.

## Why sunrise time is not a fixed number

Sunrise at Darjeeling moves by more than an hour across the year. A blog that says "leave at 4" is describing one season, not yours. The only reliable input is the sunrise time for your travel date, which you look up the week you go. The [sunrise time guide](/sunrise-time/) shows the monthly pattern and how to plan backwards from it.

## Working backwards, step by step

1. **Find the sunrise time** for Darjeeling on your date.
2. **Subtract the drive** from your hotel up to the viewpoint. It is a mountain road, so allow for it being slower than the distance suggests.
3. **Subtract a buffer** for the queue at the gate and for finding a place to stand.
4. **That is your departure hour.** Add a little if you would rather not rush.
5. **Agree it with your driver** the day before, and confirm it again when you go to bed.

If you would be disappointed to miss the first colour on the peaks, build in more cushion rather than less. The peaks colour after the sun appears, so arriving a little before is better than arriving as it happens. If you are unsure whether your buffer is enough, add another ten minutes; a slightly longer wait in the cold is a smaller regret than turning up as the best light fades.

## The night-before checklist

A calm morning is made the night before:

- **Confirm** the sunrise time and the pickup hour.
- **Write down** the meeting point and the driver's arrangement for waiting.
- **Lay out** your warm layers, shoes with grip and a torch.
- **Charge** your phone and any camera battery.
- **Pack** water and any medication you need.
- **Set** two alarms, one on your phone and one as backup.
- **Decide** your cloudy-morning fallback so you are not improvising at 5 a.m.

The [what to bring guide](/what-to-bring/) covers the kit in detail.

## Dressing for a cold, dark start

The cold before dawn is the part people underestimate. Dress in layers you can remove once daylight and the walk up warm you, with a wind-resistant outer layer. A hat and gloves make a long wait far more bearable. There is nothing to buy at the top that you can rely on, so bring what you need. See [what to bring](/what-to-bring/) for the full list.

## The queue and the last stretch

Two things eat into your buffer:

- The **queue at the gate**, which builds as everyone arrives in the same window.
- The **last stretch on foot**, walked in the dark on uneven ground.

Plan for both. Arriving is not the same as standing where you wanted to stand.

## If you are not a morning person

Be honest with yourself. If a 4 a.m. alarm tends to mean a bad mood and missed light, either commit to an early night, or accept that you may catch less of the pre-dawn build-up and simply enjoy what you see. There is no rule that you must witness the first second. The [is it worth it guide](/is-it-worth-it/) is a fair place to think that through.

## If the morning is cloudy

You have already arranged the trip, so go. Even without a clear range, the dawn light and the hill itself have their own atmosphere, and staying put wastes the arrangement. Have something to do afterwards: the [cloudy day alternatives guide](/cloudy-day-alternatives/) lists sensible fallbacks, and [breakfast after sunrise](/breakfast-after-sunrise/) gives you somewhere warm to land.
