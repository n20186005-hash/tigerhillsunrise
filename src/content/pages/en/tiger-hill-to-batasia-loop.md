## Can you add Batasia Loop to a Tiger Hill sunrise morning?

Yes, for many people Batasia Loop is the natural first stop after the sunrise. It sits on the road back down from Tiger Hill towards Darjeeling, so the vehicle that takes you up passes it on the way home. But it is a **separate place** with its own admission and its own opening conditions. Being on the same road does not mean your Tiger Hill fare or your sunrise arrangement covers it, and it does not mean the loop is open or staffed when you pass in the dark.

## Where Batasia Loop is

Batasia Loop is the spiral section of the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway and a memorial site on the road between **Ghoom and Darjeeling town**. On a sunrise trip you usually climb through Ghoom on the way up, and Batasia is lower down on the return into town, which is why it appears in almost every suggested route.

We have not verified an exact road distance, so treat any precise figure you find online as approximate and plan it as a short drive rather than a walk. The district tourism listing pairs the loop with the **Gorkha War Memorial**, set in the loop's gardens. That is what the official source describes, and we do not add history beyond it.

## What you actually see

### The spiral railway

The defining feature is the loop itself: the narrow-gauge track curves back over itself so the train can gain height on the climb. When a toy train is running through, this is the classic place to watch it circle the garden — the [Darjeeling toy train guide](/darjeeling-toy-train/) explains the railway, and the [Ghoom railway station guide](/ghoom-railway-station/) covers the nearby station. Whether a train happens to pass while you are there depends on the timetable, which we have not verified.

### The memorial and gardens

Set inside the loop is the Gorkha War Memorial, described by the district tourism source, surrounded by landscaped garden. It is a memorial, so it calls for quiet and respect rather than a photo-first stop.

### The view

On a clear morning you can look back over Darjeeling and, weather permitting, across towards the Kanchenjunga range. Nothing here guarantees a snow view: cloud can sit in the valleys and hide the range completely, and the [sunrise weather guide](/sunrise-weather/) explains why.

## Tickets and opening — what is not verified

We have not verified a current official admission fee for Batasia Loop, nor a guaranteed opening time. Before you pay, or before you rely on stopping:

- Ask whether there is a separate entry charge and exactly what it covers.
- Ask whether the garden, the train viewing and the memorial come under one ticket or several.
- Ask whether the site is actually open when you plan to pass, especially on an early sunrise morning.
- Do not treat a driver's quote as the official entry fee, and do not assume the site is free.

The [entry fee guide](/entry-fee/) lists the questions to ask about tickets on the Tiger Hill route.

## Fitting the stop into your morning

A realistic order looks like this:

1. **Before dawn** — climb to Tiger Hill for the sunrise. The [sunrise time guide](/sunrise-time/) shows how to work out when to leave.
2. **After the sun is up** — drive back down through Ghoom.
3. **On the way** — stop at Batasia Loop if it is open and you have the time.
4. **Then** — continue into Darjeeling town, or carry on to the [monastery at Ghoom](/ghoom-monastery/).

The [half-day itinerary](/half-day-itinerary/) puts this into a timed plan, and the [breakfast after sunrise guide](/breakfast-after-sunrise/) covers where to eat once you are back in town.

## Reaching it and leaving it

Most people reach Batasia Loop in the same vehicle as the Tiger Hill sunrise trip. If you are staying in Darjeeling town, some visitors approach the Ghoom side on a separate short ride, but confirm the road and the distance locally rather than trusting a figure. The [how to reach guide](/how-to-reach/) and the [parking guide](/parking/) cover vehicle logistics on the hill. Traffic around Ghoom in the morning can be slow, so build in a buffer if you have an onward train or flight.

## A word on the memorial

The Gorkha War Memorial is the reason the site is more than a railway curve. Keep voices down, do not climb on the memorial, and take photographs with care. If there is a ceremony or a crowd, give it space.

## Who might skip it

- If your only interest is the sunrise and you are cold and tired, it is fine to drive straight back.
- If you have a fixed departure, skipping the stop usually beats risking it.
- If mobility is limited, ask how close the vehicle can park to the garden entrance; the [accessibility guide](/accessibility/) lists the questions to ask.
